Paper-by-paper analysis

What each paper contributes and where this work goes further

Egger et al. (2020) IBM Quantum Experience Credit risk · 5 assets · 5 qubits

First demonstration of quantum amplitude estimation for financial risk on real hardware. Estimated credit-risk loss distributions for a 5-asset portfolio on IBMQ, achieving a proof-of-concept with a 5-qubit circuit. Circuit width scales linearly with N, making this approach impractical for realistic portfolio sizes.

Their contribution
First real-HW QAE for credit risk. Established IBMQ as viable platform for finance experiments.
This work adds
Fixed 4-qubit width for any N via ratio estimator. QUBO universe selection. Basel IV validation.
Stamatopoulos et al. (2020) IBM Quantum Experience Option pricing · ≤7 assets · depth <20

Demonstrated quantum amplitude estimation for option pricing on IBMQ hardware, achieving circuit depth below 20 for up to 7 assets. Established the shallow-circuit regime as the practical target for near-term quantum finance applications. No portfolio optimisation loop — only risk estimation, not CVaR minimisation.

Their contribution
Shallow QAE circuits (depth <20) on real hardware. Demonstrated amplitude estimation accuracy.
This work adds
Full hybrid optimisation loop. Explicit aliasing characterisation depth(m)=4+6m. IQM hardware (not IBMQ). Scales to N=100.
IBM – Vanguard (2023) Real hardware Portfolio risk · 40+ assets · Circuit scales with N

Industry collaboration demonstrating quantum portfolio risk analysis for 40+ assets on IBMQ hardware. Largest N demonstrated on real quantum hardware at time of publication. However, circuit width grows with N, requires many qubits for large portfolios, and no integration with classical optimisation or regulatory validation was reported.

Their contribution
Largest real-HW portfolio (40+ assets). Industry validation of quantum risk analysis at scale.
This work adds
Fixed 4-qubit width — does NOT grow with N. End-to-end CVaR optimisation. Basel IV ES backtesting. QUBO universe selection.
Laudäge-Turkalj (2025) Theory only CVaR vs EVaR · No hardware · Any N

Theoretical comparison of CVaR and Entropic VaR (EVaR) estimation under quantum amplitude estimation. Derives tight complexity bounds for both risk measures and shows EVaR provides a coherent upper bound on CVaR. No hardware experiments; no optimisation loop. Provides the theoretical framework used in this work’s EVaR estimation module.

Their contribution
Rigorous complexity analysis of CVaR and EVaR under QAE. Establishes EVaR ≥ CVaR coherence hierarchy.
This work adds
Hardware validation of those bounds. IQAE aliasing threshold characterisation. CVaR optimisation loop with regulatory backtesting.
Skarlatos (2025) Theory only CVaR subgradient · O(Nε−1) · 4-qubit

Closest concurrent theoretical work. Proves the O(Nε−1) subgradient complexity using a fixed-width IQAE circuit — the same 4-qubit framework implemented here. Shows theoretically that the circuit width is independent of N via ratio estimation. No hardware validation, no noise characterisation, no optimisation loop, no real data.

Their contribution
Proves O(Nε−1) complexity. Fixed 4-qubit width theory. Establishes the theoretical framework this work implements.
This work adds
First hardware validation of Skarlatos’ framework on IQM. Empirical aliasing threshold. QUBO universe selection. Basel IV. 46 systematic experiments.
Honest assessment

When does the quantum approach actually help?

The results of this paper allow a concrete answer rather than a theoretical one. Three conditions must hold simultaneously.

(i)
Sufficient portfolio size

O(Nε−1) oracle calls must be fewer than the O(Nε−2) classical Monte Carlo cost. This activates at ε < 0.05 for any N. At the ε=0.005 target: quantum requires 200× fewer oracle calls per asset.

(ii)
Sufficient gradient spread

The selected asset universe must have meaningful marginal CVaR spread. Empirically: ≥3× spread between max and min gradient components. PA achieves 5.70×; PB only 1.94× — the QUBO selection is decisive.

(iii)
Hardware fidelity below aliasing

Gate fidelity must be sufficient to avoid IQAE aliasing within the required round count. Currently limits hardware IQAE to εhw ≈ 0.20. Conditions (i)–(ii) are met; condition (iii) constrains practical advantage to simulation today.

Current status: conditions (i) and (ii) are satisfied. Condition (iii) is not yet met on current NISQ hardware (εhw ≈ 0.20 vs target ε = 0.005). The pipeline is correct and regulatory-compliant today; the classical speed advantage requires hardware gate fidelity improvements estimated at 2028–2030 on industry roadmaps. Cosine similarity >0.9999 in all 46 experiments confirms the gradient direction is already reliable.
Gaps in the literature

Three gaps this work addresses

Gap G1
No aliasing characterisation

No prior work identified or characterised the IQAE aliasing threshold — the circuit depth beyond which measurements become unreliable on IQM superconducting processors.

Addressed: depth(m)=4+6m · aliasing at m=3 confirmed on Garnet and Emerald
Gap G2
No integrated universe selection

Quantum CVaR estimators were applied to arbitrarily chosen universes. No work integrated market-network-based asset selection (QUBO) with quantum gradient estimation in a single pipeline.

Addressed: QUBO peripherality selection on S&P 500 MST · D-Wave · 10/11 communities
Gap G3
No regulatory validation

Basel IV ES backtesting of quantum-optimised portfolios — particularly the Acerbi-Székely test — had not been reported in any prior work.

Addressed: Acerbi-Székely PASS at all α · capital buffer 50% vs 75% classical
